Q05Fine-tuning Legal DomainHard
You fine-tuned GPT-3.5 on legal documents for ATARI Group. For a BBVA deployment requiring AI to accurately handle financial regulatory documents (MiFID II, Basel III compliance text), what is the key risk of fine-tuning compared to RAG, and when would you choose fine-tuning?
AFine-tuning risks are purely computational — it is more expensive than RAG; choose fine-tuning only when the budget exceeds RAG infrastructure costs
BFine-tuning on proprietary regulatory documents creates copyright liability — RAG cites sources explicitly, while fine-tuning absorbs document content into model weights without attribution

Q21Semantic Search ImplementationMedium
In your procurement matching system you built 3-stage matching: SKU match → exact name → semantic similarity. For a BBVA financial product recommendation system (matching customer needs to BBVA products), which similarity metric is most appropriate for semantic search over financial product descriptions?
ACosine similarity — it measures the angle between embedding vectors, making it invariant to vector magnitude. Financial product descriptions of different lengths produce embeddings of varying magnitudes.
BDot product — it is always faster than cosine similarity and produces identical ranking results for all embedding models; cosine similarity is only needed for unormalised embeddings
CEuclidean distance — it measures the absolute difference between embedding vectors and works well for financial text where document length is uniform
DJaccard similarity — it measures the overlap between term sets and is more accurate than vector similarity for financial terminology which relies on precise matching of regulatory terms
